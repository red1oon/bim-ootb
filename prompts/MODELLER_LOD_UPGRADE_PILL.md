**What is ported (same maths, same order, no clock, no random, every loop over sorted keys):**
1. Per plate: a frame from the minimum-area rectangle of its in-plane triangle edges (thickness axis = smallest world extent); the plate box in that frame.
2. Per member: centroid + long axis (largest-eigenvalue direction of the vertex covariance, sign fixed by the largest component), its end positions along that axis.
3. Contact member x plate: bounding boxes first; then triangle-box separating-axis test (Akenine-Moller) plus a ray-parity test for the plate-box corners inside the member mesh; the contact interval along the member axis = the member triangles clipped to the plate box (Sutherland-Hodgman, 6 planes). Interval shorter than 1e-6 m = touch only. End contact (A, B or AB) when the interval reaches within `weld_contact_end_tol_mm` of a member end, otherwise THROUGH (T).
4. Weld = two triangular fillet beads (8 triangles each, 24 vertices) laid on the +face of the plate along the two outer edges of the member section, length = the overlap interval. Size = AISC 360-16 Table J2.4 minimum for the thinner part (plate thickness read from its name, else measured; member leg thickness read from its name). Bead end caps are wound outward from the bead's own centroid (the 2026-11-18 correction).
5. Refused, never dropped: a contact whose member axis is not parallel to the plate plane (|axis . plate normal| > 0.05) gets `REFUSED ... member axis not parallel to the plate plane ...` (the 26 perpendicular "Section run" contacts on the Shed); a member that does not cross the plate-face plane gets `... no seam`. ## P3 SPEC (2026-11-18) — Layer split (pack `layers`, label "Layers")
**Issue it proves or disproves:** *"the browser result differs from the proven Python result"* for the layer split of the extractor (BIMCompiler `DAGCompiler/python/extractIFCtoDB.py`: `compile_layer_geometry` L1403, `_layer_intervals` L1330, `_slice_slab` L1290, `_cross_section_loops` L1148, `_cap_triangles` L1213, `_orient_coherently` L1066, refusals `§LAYER-REFUSE` L1033/L1440, and the DB writer `component_geometry_layers` L325/L1579) as consumed by `~/Projects/Jebel/lod_sandbox/jpam/build_jpam.py` section A (part names, change rows, frame). Oracle output: the JPAM DB (220 `GEN-LY-*` parts, 9 `REFUSED ... Layer split` rows).

**What the source DB really carries (measured on the ARC extract):** the extractor compiled the split in place. For the 88 split hosts the stored host mesh already IS the concatenated layer slabs (same hash key as before), indexed by `component_geometry_layers` (`layer_seq`, `material_name`, `thickness_m`, `face_start`, `face_count`); the 9 refused hosts still hold their one envelope solid. So the pack reads the DB in two honest states, never mixing them:
1. **Sliced state** (the host's hash has index rows): each part = the triangles `face_start .. +face_count` of the host mesh, but only after the pack re-verifies them against the authored tables (never trusts the index blindly): stated thickness equals the slab extent along the layer axis within `layer_tol_mm`; the slab's span along the axis equals the authored interval for that layer (the extractor's `_layer_intervals`, absolute else relative anchoring, direction sense applied); each slab watertight with positive volume.
2. **Envelope state** (no index rows): the port of the slicer itself: weld (1e-9 m), edge-manifold check, coherent outward winding, signed volume, authored intervals, Sutherland-Hodgman clip of the crossing triangles, caps from the cross-section loops (edge-keyed chaining, even-odd nesting for holes), weld, watertight proof, per-slab extent proof, volume re-sum proof. Differences from the oracle, stated: cap triangulation is ear clipping with hole bridges instead of GEOS constrained Delaunay. Both use only the loop points (no Steiner points) so the vertex SET and every area are identical; the triangle fan inside a cap may differ. The witness therefore compares vertex sets and volumes, not triangle order, for this state. Self-intersecting loops are not tested (the oracle's `is_valid`); a cap whose triangulation fails or whose area differs is a REFUSED, never a guess.

**Refusals, never dropped, reason text identical to the extractor's (`§LAYER-REFUSE`):** `LayerSetDirection missing/unknown (None)`, `DirectionSense missing/unknown`, authored total missing, `material_layers has N rows ...`, a layer thickness missing, thicknesses do not re-sum, envelope not watertight / not orientable / nested cavity / zero volume, `body span [a,b] (extent e) does not align with the authored layer set (total t, offset o, sense s) under absolute OR relative anchoring`, slab extent / volume / empty-slab proofs. Order of the checks = the Python order. Each becomes a change row `REFUSED` with the reason; the host stays visible (no part is written, so P1's seeding keeps it).

**Parts:** `GEN-LY-<host>-<seq>`, class `IfcBuildingElementPart`, name `<material> <th*1000:.1f> mm (layer <seq> of <set>)`, host storey, host colour, world mesh = host transform (Euler rx,ry,rz as in the extractor's `R_recon`) applied to the local slab, own bbox centre (spec §5), provenance `generated:layers`, `parts_of:[host]` (so `element_parts` host -> part is derived and P1's seeding hides the host; the oracle writes exactly these rows). Change row ADDED: rule `Layer split along IfcMaterialLayerSetUsage <AXIS>`, detail `host=<cls> "<name>" | layer <seq> <material> | stated <th> mm | measured <m> mm | storey <s>` (the oracle's text). A layer the authored geometry clips away has no part and no row (extractor ruling 2026-07-31) and is logged `§LODUP_LAYER_CLIP`.

**Rules as data:** `lod/rules/layer_defaults.json`: `layer_tol_mm` 0.5 (the extractor's `LAYER_TOL`), `layer_sum_tol_mm` 0.001 (`LAYER_SUM_TOL`), `layer_volume_rtol` 0.0001 (`LAYER_VOL_RTOL`), `layer_min_count` 2 (`layer_count > 1`); every row `default:`. No project value.

**Engine change (generic, additive):** `lod_reader.js` gains `layers` (hosts, material_layers, host mesh from `component_geometries` else `base_geometries`, the layer index, transform) and `rgba` on `meta`; `world()` is untouched. `lod_ui.js` is NOT edited (sha256 asserted); one line in `packs.json`; `modeller.html` is not edited either (the dialog loads packs from `packs.json`).

**Frame:** the Modeller shows the extractor frame (`world = R(rx,ry,rz) v + centre`). The oracle parts live in the combined frame (extractor frame + one fixed translation: `site_normalization` offset minus a measured shift). **Where the JS cannot copy the Python, and what replaces it (stated up front, not tuned afterwards):**
- *The section profile.* The oracle looks the web thickness, flange thickness and root fillet up in the IFC (`IfcIShapeProfileDef`); the DB does not carry them. A section is a **rolled I-section** when it has such a web pair (thickness at most `web_width_fraction_max` of the width) AND root-fillet faces (>= `root_fillet_faces_min`); a welded outline with no fillet and any non-I section is refused as "not a rolled I-section", which is the oracle's "not an I-section in the IFC profiles" (21 ends). - *Eigenvector signs.* The end letter (A/B) and the order of the rays depend on the sign a solver returns. Fixed by convention: the axis' first component larger than 1e-9 is negative (numpy returned exactly that on all 117 members), depth and width: largest component positive. Ends are matched to the oracle by end point.
- *Ties.* The plate end often sits exactly half way between two 2 mm samples. A tie is decided by a micrometre of noise, so the rule is fixed: within 1 micrometre the deeper sample wins; every joint's tie margin is kept in the audit and listed by the witness. Deviations and findings (none hidden):
1. **The tie rule was decided after seeing a result.** The plate front follows the support face at the sample nearest the plate end; the samples lie on a 2 mm lattice and for 42 of the 50 joints the plate end is exactly half way between two samples. The first parity run used "lower sample wins" and moved the plate and weld front face of those 42 joints by 0.02-0.036 mm (one 2 mm step times the support's skew), outside the 0.01 mm limit (the 8 non-tie joints were identical); every one of the oracle's own picks was the deeper sample. The rule was then fixed to "within 1 micrometre the deeper sample wins" and the witness prints the tie count; the rule is the only thing changed between the two runs. It is a tie-break convention, not a tuned number, but it was chosen with the oracle in view.
2. **The IFC section profile is gone, replaced by the mesh.** Web thickness, clear web half height (= D/2 - tf - r) and "is this a rolled I-section" are read from the end mesh. The clear web half height equals the IFC profile's to 0.001 mm on all 50 joints. Of the 21 ends refused as not-rolled-I, 16 are welded built-up outlines (12 vertices, no root fillet: `BU-500x300x50x25` and the `BU BOX HSS 200X75X12` family) that ARE I-shapes in the mesh and 5 are angles; the 16 are refused because the oracle never detailed them (its profile table holds only rolled sections), which `rolled_section_only` states as a rule row. The web-thickness-vs-IFC clause cannot be tested and is dropped (8 of the oracle's 9 clauses remain).
3. **Eigenvector signs are a convention** (axis: first component larger than 1e-9 negative; numpy returned that on all 117 members). It fixes the end letter (all 234 equal the oracle's) and the ray order.
4. **CLEAR_POST skips a point outside a neighbour's own box** (a closed mesh cannot contain it; on an open mesh the parity test could answer "odd" far away). It halves the cost; results equal on this model (0 failures both ways).
5. **The steel member set is the oracle's name rule plus a material gate** (`member_name_include / exclude`, `member_material_gate` rows). 3,586 other IfcBeam elements in the model carry a steel material, so the material gate alone would admit them; the name include / exclude rules are what keep them out (117 members, as the oracle). A model named differently needs its own rows (stated).
6. **Cost.** 9-10 s a run on this 4.7 M-vertex model, mostly ray-parity tests of generated parts against neighbours up to 20,000 triangles; the survey (1-2 s) is all the chooser needs. Big meshes (> 4,000 triangles, 5 of them over the parity limit: cables, mesh, coping, curtain, PV cables) are read through a triangle grid and never expanded.
7. **The live scene check could not read the beams' own meshes** (this DB opens through the local-file route, where `__arcFidByGuid` has no beam entries): the parts drew (150 meshes, 108,360 vertices) and no `element_parts` / `parts_of` row exists, so the host-hide rule (which reads only `parts_of`) cannot hide a beam; beam visibility itself was not read.

**Reused, not duplicated (P4, `joint_pack.js`):** the survey (rays from the beam ends, STEEL_SKEW class), the TRUE-box spatial index, the mesh reader with the triangle grid, the ray / ray-parity primitives, the eigenframe and its sign conventions, the J2.4 weld table. They are exposed once as `LodJointPack.kit`; `gusset_pack.js` requires it (`packs.json` lists `joints` before `gussets`). `appliesTo` reads the survey through one cache shared with the joints pack (keyed on the model and on the rows the survey reads), so the chooser surveys once.

**What is ported (same maths, same order, no clock, no random, every loop over sorted keys):**
1. **Per STEEL_SKEW end:** brace frame (covariance eigenframe, end by the stored end point); the end cap (triangles lying in the end plane) and its area centroid = the brace gravity axis; **eccentricity** = distance between that axis and the support axis, MEASURED, REPORTED, never corrected (AISC 360-16 J1.1; the support axis is the support's vertex mean, so it is approximate for an asymmetric section). A cap with fewer than `end_cap_min_triangles` is refused `NO_SECTION`.
2. **Leg plane (§8.1):** the two legs from the 4th-power direction fit of the cap boundary; the one within `plane_tol_deg` of the plane (brace axis, support axis) is the gusset plane, the other must be outside it, else `AMBIGUOUS_PLANE`. The gusset sits on the face of that leg AWAY from the outstanding leg; the leg thickness is measured on the end cap (not read from the name).
3. **Attachment:** slice the support at the leg mid-plane; the face the leg plane meets within `support_reach_mm` (else `NO_ATTACHMENT`); flange if the material interval along the plate normal is <= `flange_interval_max_fraction` x section depth (a lap of `max(5 x thinner, lap_min_mm)`, two fillets along the lapped edges), else a web / box wall (a T joint, fillets on both faces; wall must be <= `support_wall_max_mm`). The gusset is placed touching the support; the gap to the angle leg is a **filler** tagged `<= 6 mm` (AISC J5.2: no shear reduction) or `> 6 mm: engineer to check (J5.2)`; filler > `filler_max_mm` 25 is refused `GAP_TOO_LARGE`; a gusset lower than the leg face may enter the angle by less than its own thickness, more is `LEG_OVERLAP`.
4. **Layout:** bolt line at the leg centre line, `bolts_min` bolts at `bolt_pitch_mm`, first bolt `edge_used_mm` from the contact edge; a first bolt closer than `edge_min_mm` to the angle end is `NO_ROOM`. Plate thickness = max(leg thickness, `gusset_min_thickness_mm`); weld = J2.4 for the thinner part.
5. **Clash against EVERY neighbour (TRUE boxes, never the DB rtree):** exact separating-axis test of every part (plate, each bolt prism, each weld bead) against every neighbour triangle, plus the swallowed-whole ray-parity test (neighbours up to `parity_max_triangles`; larger ones surface-tested only and listed). The brace is exempt (bolted angle); the support is exempt only for the welds and, for a lap, the plate (the plate then bears on the flange face; a T plate is tested pulled back `clash_tolerance_mm` from the support face). Neighbours exclude other packs' duplicates of their hosts (`GEN-LY-`, `GEN-RB-`, earlier `GEN-GU-` are tested as generated parts instead). A clash refuses the joint, `REFUSED_CLASH`, with what it hit.
6. **Clause check** (14 clauses measured on the built meshes: pitch, hole spacing, plate and angle edge distances, 12t / 150, weld min and max, plate thickness, bolts inside the angle leg, contact gap, filler, bolt count) and **CLEAR_POST** on the parts rebuilt from the op-log rows (every part's surface vs every other solid; brace exempt; support exempt only where welded).
