R0

Stock-take

Keep the format, prove it works on sets we didn't make. Small firms need it. The format is sound and differs from the AI plan readers. What is not yet shown is that it works on drawings we didn't write, and the effort is still too high.

Will small firms look at it?

The need is real. Small firms are still mostly in 2D: a 2025 survey of 77 Nigerian firms, and a UK survey where more than half of practices under £1M turnover had not used BIM Level 2. Bricsys (a vendor) says 2D arrives on 40–50% of its users' projects. Small firms buy a deliverable, not "BIM": quantities, an IFC for a submission, or a 3D model for a client.

What would stop them today: 47 actions on the shed, 151 on the Duplex (82% of them doors and windows), datums typed as CAD coordinates, and our own vocabulary (@CUT, @HAXIS).

Is the 2D → 3D format a good baseline?

  • It reads several sheets together, so heights come from the elevations and sections. The AI readers ask for heights by hand.
  • Every value carries its tier: read default inferred ask. A QS or a submission can see which numbers are guesses.
  • The tiers don't care where a suggestion comes from, so ML suggestions can be added later as yellow or orange without breaking the "never silent" rule.
  • The markings are saved inside the DXF, the result exports to IFC4, and the Viewer round trip is verified (512 = 512).

R1–R2

The HHS round trip, step by step

The HHS model (architecture plus structure columns, 133 doors, 112 walls, 33 curtain walls) is written as our standard 2D set with the Find-panel Room Injection rooms: 9 sheets, 673 markings and 73 room labels. The markings are then stripped and the plain sheets go back in. At each step we add only what a user would have to state, taking every value from the model's own markings (never invented), and measure what is left.

Step 0Accept all only

29 accepted. 8 of 9 sheets get no role: our own titles end in  1:100 and the title rule wants them to end in PLAN, ELEVATION or SECTION. Duplex: 7 of 8.

Step 1+ 8 roles, set name

172 accepted, 167 match the model: units, faces, axes, all 25 level datums, 123 of 160 grid lines. 5 wrong: @LEVEL=0 on all three plans (the model says 0.094 / 3.8 / 7.3), the section cut taken from grid bubble A, the name including the scale. The gate catches the duplicate level, so the wrong values are caught, not silent.

Step 2+ 52 sheet facts

487 blocking, mostly walls no elevation shows (366). Two kinds of fact turn out unnecessary (names, defaults: same verdict without them); layer kinds and the minimum wall thickness are needed.

Step 3+ the whole key, 506

166 blocking. All of them are known engine or library items (71 need the library in the browser, 45 are library-bound host cuts, 30 are doors hinged 43 mm off their partition). Duplex reaches 36, all library. 401 of the 506 statements are per-wall tops (268) and per-door tags (133).

R3

What the round trip teaches

The drawing teaches its own convention

220 of the 268 HHS wall tops (82%) sit exactly at the next level minus 0.300 m, the slab the section shows. One relation, learned from the walls the drawing does measure, answers the walls it doesn't, as an inferred value with "k of n walls agree" as its evidence. On the Duplex the walls agree only 50%, so the rule must abstain there. That is the falsifier, and it fires.

Decide per group, not per element

Door leaf width predicts the door type perfectly on both sets (HHS: 10 widths, 10 types; Duplex: 4 and 4). Tagging by width turns 133 per-door tags into 10 schedule rows, entered once for the set rather than per sheet.

Read what the sheets already say

The plain sheets already carry (FFL +3.800), ROOF LEVEL (+10.900), T/ROOF +10.695 and AT X=-8682.15. Read as read values, they give every role, level, roof and cut: 19 asks on HHS go to 0, and the 4 wrong values disappear (Duplex: 17 asks, 2 wrong values).

Room Injection and doors

Measured: rooms do not settle door types (room pair alone 76%, no gain over width), and only 25 of 133 doors have a room on both sides. Rooms help elsewhere: from/to columns in a door schedule, floor finishes per room, and a sanity check on the slab.

Statements, not timed actions. The page's pooled layer choice and set-wide set name are counted once. Left over: 48 wall tops, 10 schedule rows, 37 grid lines, 3 layer kinds, 3 minimum thicknesses, 1 set name.

Your last job is your library. A type library learned on one building answers nothing on the other: no door width is shared within 1 mm, and the door heights are 2260 vs 2032 mm. What transfers is the rules. The common library is of conventions, and the type and material catalogue is per firm, learned by round-tripping that firm's own past models.
